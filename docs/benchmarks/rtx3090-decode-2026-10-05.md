# RTX 3090 packed decode: SM86 direct trial

The RTX 8000 four-row PQ2/Q8 DP4A decode kernel was built for SM86 and tested directly on the RTX 3090. It is not a universal 3090 winner: the initial four-row kernel lost 29.5% to Prism on the sum of three batch-2 matrix shapes. A measured SM86 selection now uses two rows per warp for batches 1 and 4, four rows for batch 8, and Prism for batch 2. The production endpoint was not changed or tested.

The final selected runtime passed 23/23 `test-backend-ops` CPU-reference cases, including all twelve full-size shape/batch cases and small/tail/fusion controls. Its dispatch log records native `pq2_q8_1_dp4a_warp` for all full-size batch 1/4/8 cases and `prism` with `unsupported_decode_shape_or_fusion` for all three batch-2 cases. All tests used the same staged SM86 runtime on `.42`; the build ran on `.30` without a container. Robot `/health.running_runs.total` was 0 before each build and GPU test.

The unweighted sum of these three operator timings improved 6.8% at batch 1, 18.3% at batch 4, and 12.1% at batch 8; batch 2 stayed within 0.3% of stock. That sum is **not** a model-token estimate: these matrices are not necessarily called equally often, and attention, quantization and the rest of the graph are excluded. This is one paired graph-replay benchmark per shape, not repeated independent trials. The proposal's pass criterion of at least 10% on batches 1, 4 and 8 on both large shapes was **not met**; several individual large-shape gains are only 2–8%.

The variant sweep explains the selection. With the original four-row SM86 mapping, the three-shape sums versus Prism were +0.2% at batch 1, **−29.5%** at batch 2, +8.1% at batch 4 and +12.0% at batch 8. Two rows yielded +6.8%, −9.0%, +18.5% and +4.1%. One row yielded +3.8%, −30.8%, −19.6% and −52.7%. Every variant passed 23/23 correctness cases. The two-row batch-2 loss and one-row regression disprove the idea that reducing row count alone fixes batch 2. The selected code keeps the proven four-row mapping on SM75, preserving the previously measured RTX 8000 path.

The first clean SM86 build was standalone engine revision `176039a` with `--cuda-arch 86`; it supplied the exact stock and four-row control. Subsequent row variants were compiled incrementally from its isolated pinned-source build directory, with the changed `merlin-decode.cuh` copied from this repository. The `.42` stage manifest still names `176039a` and **must not be treated as a manifest for the later scratch variant**. The final selected library was saved at `/home/oscar/merlin-engine-candidates/rtx3090-selected-sm86-2026-10-05.so`, and the stage's original four-row library was restored to match its manifest. Raw timings, correctness output and dispatch JSONL are in [the evidence directory](rtx3090-decode-2026-10-05-evidence/).

The other proposed levers have different evidence. The [RTX 8000 F16-KV A/B](rtx8000-f16-kv-ab-2026-10-05.md) improved decode time 21% at 61K and 32% at 128K, but whole requests only 0.4% and 1.0%; it does not improve prefill. The earlier claimed sustained 212 ms/token at 128K was based on a ten-token decode and did not reproduce over 128 tokens. The proposed PQ2 repack and Turing attention tile change are not established fixes: the measured PRMT source attribution does not quantify a repack gain, and the attention MMA kernel masks the two spare GQA columns. Neither was coded. P40 work remains deferred until the 3090 target is resolved. No SpecKit or production endpoint change was made.
